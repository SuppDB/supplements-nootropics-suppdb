The most common supplement ingredients

Vitamin C is on 18.6% of supplement labels, Calcium on 16.7% and Magnesium on 16.6%. The most common botanical is Ashwagandha at 8.0%.

What kinds of ingredients supplements contain

41.8% of labels list at least one botanical ingredient, 39.6% a vitamin and 39.5% a mineral. Probiotic bacteria appear on 8.1% of labels.

How often the dose is hidden

24.6% of supplement labels use a proprietary blend, which lists ingredients without their individual amounts. As a result 37.7% of all 115,051 ingredient listings carry no per-ingredient dose. Liquid products hide doses most often (31.2%), Softgel Capsule least (8.1%).

How doses compare with reference intakes

Per serving, 91.9% of Vitamin B12 listings exceed the adult daily reference intake, as do 88.8% of Vitamin B6 and 84.3% of Biotin listings. Against the tolerable upper intake level, the line most often crossed is Niacin: 30.1% of its dosed listings exceed the adult UL per serving, followed by Magnesium (19.5%) and Zinc (9.7%).

Forms and formula size

35.2% of products are capsules, 19.3% powders and 15.7% tablets or pills. 36.4% of products contain a single active ingredient; the median product has 2, the mean 6.5, and the most complex formula lists 206. A container holds a median of 50 servings.

Two ingredients up close: magnesium and caffeine

Magnesium Oxide is the most common magnesium form at 15.9% of magnesium listings, ahead of Magnesium Citrate (14.3%) and Magnesium Glycinate (7.6%); 14.4% of listings name no salt at all. Caffeine is dosed on 240 labels at a median of 150 mg per serving (mean 163 mg, maximum 400 mg); 56 of them exceed 200 mg in one serving.

Method, reuse and citation

  • Source. The full SuppDB snapshot of 2026-07-07: 17,625 product labels from the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (U.S. Government, public domain), each with its DSLD label ID and URL. Active ingredients are normalised to milligrams per serving; chemistry for 4,111 of 16,566 distinct ingredients comes from NIH PubChem.
  • Nothing is estimated. Amounts, forms, blend flags and serving counts are the label's own values as recorded by DSLD. Where a label states no amount the listing is excluded from dose figures, and every section states its denominator.
  • Labels, not sales. DSLD holds labels submitted or collected by NIH, so shares describe what is on the market's labels, not what is sold or consumed, and reference-intake comparisons are per serving as labelled.
  • Refresh. SuppDB is rebuilt from the DSLD manifest on a monthly cadence; this page and its charts are regenerated with each snapshot, so figures move. Cite the snapshot date.
  • Reuse. The figures and charts on this page are published under CC BY 4.0: use them in articles, slides and posts with a link to https://suppdb.dataengineered.io/stats/. The machine-readable version is data.json. The underlying row-level catalogue is a separate commercial product; a free sample is in the public repository.
  • Suggested citation. SuppDB (2026). Dietary supplements in numbers, snapshot 2026-07-07. DataEngineered. https://suppdb.dataengineered.io/stats/
